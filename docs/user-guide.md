# Using Job Search HQ

This guide is for the person doing the job search. Someone has already set up your copy (that's [install.md](install.md)), and all you need is a browser. It works the same on a Chromebook, a computer or a phone.

**What it does:** it keeps your job search in one place. It collects jobs from your email alerts, scores how well each one fits you, writes a tailored resume and cover letter from your real experience, helps you reach people at the company, and lets you practice interview answers. You stay in charge: it never applies for you, never sends email, and never posts anything.

## 1. Getting in

1. Open your address, which looks like `https://pat.203-0-113-10.sslip.io`.
2. Choose **Sign in with Google**, using the Google account your copy was set up for. No other account can open it.
3. **Make it an app** so it's one click away:
   - **Chromebook or computer:** in Chrome's address bar, choose the **Install** icon. Job Search HQ then opens in its own window.
   - **iPhone or iPad:** in Safari, tap **Share**, then **Add to Home Screen**. Open it from the home screen from then on.
   - **Android:** in Chrome, tap the menu **⋮**, then **Install app** (or **Add to Home screen** on some versions).

   [devices.md, Part 2](devices.md#part-2-using-job-search-hq-on-each-device) has step-by-step instructions for Windows, Mac, Chromebook, iPhone and Android, including notifications and the microphone.

The top of every page has the menu: Today, Roles, Chat, Profile, Interview, Documents, Outreach, Practice, Inbox, Reminders and Suggestions. A number next to Suggestions means facts are waiting for your approval. Setup, Settings and **Sign out** are at the end.

**Messages on screen:** after you save or change something, a short message appears at the bottom of the screen, such as "Saved." Green messages fade on their own. Red ones mean something went wrong: they stay until you close them with **×**, so there's time to read them. [Section 13](#13-if-something-goes-wrong) explains the common ones.

## 2. Your first hour: the Setup page

Open **Setup** and work down it. Each item explains itself, and Today shows a reminder until they're done.

1. **Connect Claude.** Claude is the AI that does the writing and scoring. Usually the person who set up your copy has done this already. If not, see [Settings](#12-settings-and-your-data).
2. **Connect Gmail (read only).**
   - Google shows a "Google hasn't verified this app" screen. That's expected for a private app on your own server. Choose **Continue**. If you only see **Back to safety**, choose **Advanced**, then the **Go to … (unsafe)** link. Google names the app after your address, for example sslip.io, not Job Search HQ.
   - **Tick the box** that allows reading your email, then **Continue**.
   - Job Search HQ can only read. It can't send, delete or label anything, and it only looks at job alerts and emails about jobs.
   - **About once a week, Google disconnects it.** That's how Google treats private apps like this one. You get a notification, and Today shows **Reconnect Gmail**. One tap reconnects it.
3. **Set up job alerts.**
   - **LinkedIn:** search Jobs for a title you want, set the filters, turn on **Set alert**, and choose Daily and Email. Repeat for each title.
   - **Indeed:** search for the same titles and choose **Get new jobs for this search by email**.
   - Then choose **I've set up alerts**. The alerts go to your Gmail, and Job Search HQ picks them up from there.
4. **Notifications on this device.** Choose **Turn on notifications**, then **Send a test**. Do this on each device you want notified on. On an iPhone, open the app from the home screen first.
5. **Watch company job boards** (optional). Add companies you'd love to work for on the Inbox page, and their new openings appear each morning.
6. **Free trials** (optional). If your copy runs on free trials, enter their start dates so you're reminded before anything bills.

The last part of the page, **For whoever runs your server**, is for the person who set up your copy. You can skip it.

## 3. Your profile

Everything the app writes comes from your profile, so the more real detail it has, the better your resumes and letters are. It never invents experience.

The **Profile** page has these parts, top to bottom:

- **Basic information:** your name, contact details, the job titles you want, your target level, the work modes you'd accept, pay and deal-breakers. Choose **Save basic information** when you're done.
  - **Pay:** type it the way you think of it. Hourly (`18/hr`), monthly (`3,000 a month`) and yearly (`45k a year`, or just `45k`) all work, in any currency (`€45k`, `C$60k`, `£30,000 a year`). Hourly pay is compared with yearly pay at full time (2,080 hours a year), and the page shows the yearly amount under what you typed. If you type a bare number like `18`, the app guesses the unit and says what it read ("read 18 as $18 an hour"). Add the unit if it guessed wrong.
  - **Longest commute for on-site:** miles (`15`) or kilometers (`25 km`). On-site jobs farther away fail the location check.
  - **Anything else the AI should know:** for example the shifts you can work, or a career break you'd like handled with care. The AI reads it when it scores jobs and writes for you.
- **Work history:** choose **Add a job** for each job you've had: company, title, dates and a one-line summary. Then, under each job, **Add fact** for specific results, ideally with numbers ("Cut month-end close from 10 days to 6").
- **Other facts:** awards, volunteer work, languages and anything else that isn't tied to one job.
- **Education:** school, degree, field and dates.
- **Certifications and licenses:** licenses, certifications and training a job may ask for, like a CNA license, CPR, a forklift certification or an AWS certification. Add the name, who issued it and the date. Every one appears on your resume, written as you entered it.
- **Projects:** school, personal or volunteer projects that show what you can do, with what you did and what came of it. The AI picks the ones that fit each role. They help most when your work history is short.
- **Skills and tools:** add each one as **I have it** or **Never used**. A tool marked never used is never claimed in your documents.

**Early in your career?** If you have no jobs yet, only part-time, intern, seasonal or volunteer jobs, or a target level like "entry level", your resume puts education, certifications and projects before work history.

Two more pages build your profile:
- **Interview page:** the AI asks you questions to draw out experience you haven't written down. Answer in your own words, as if talking to a friend. It also asks about tools that the jobs you've saved mention: answer, choose **Never used it**, or **Skip**.
- **Suggestions page:** the facts the AI picked out from your answers wait here. Edit the wording if you like, then **Approve** the ones that are right and **Reject** the rest. Nothing goes into your profile until you approve it.

## 4. Where jobs come from

Jobs come in these ways, and all of them land on **Roles** as "saved":

- **Your email alerts:** every hour from 7 AM to 9 PM, it reads new LinkedIn and Indeed alert emails and adds the jobs.
- **Company job boards:** each morning, it checks the companies you're watching.
- **Adding one yourself:** on Roles, choose **Add a role**. Fill in the company and title, plus a link if you have one. For LinkedIn jobs, also paste the posting text into **Posting text**, since LinkedIn pages need a login the app doesn't have. If you know someone there who could refer you, add them under **Someone who can refer you**. With posting text and Claude connected, the role is scored on its own.
- **A role with no posting,** such as one a recruiter or a friend told you about: add it with what you know in **Notes** (pay, hours, place, what the job involves). Then open it and choose **Score fit**. The score uses the title, the company and your notes, and says "Scored without a posting". Anything your notes don't cover shows as unknown.

## 5. Today and Roles

**Today** is the page to open each morning. It shows:
- **Due now:** follow-ups, reminders and anything with a date today.
- **Ready to apply:** roles that score well and pass your basic checks (location, pay, the kind of employer and industry).
- **In progress:** roles you've applied to, are interviewing for, or have an offer from.
- **Needs a look:** profile suggestions waiting for you, emails to review, and questions about tools.

**Roles** lists everything, sorted into lanes: **Ready to apply**, **Your call** (it fails a check, or needs a judgment only you can make), **Needs checking** (something is unknown, or it isn't scored yet), **Later** and **Expired**. Open a role to:
- **Score fit:** the AI compares the posting with your profile and gives a score out of 100 with its reasons, plus a pass, fail or unknown for each check.
- **Change the status** with the buttons under **Status**: Saved, Applied, Interviewing, Offer, Accepted, Rejected, Withdrawn or Skipped. Each button saves right away. **Change history**, at the bottom of the page, has **Undo**.
- **Do something:** **Tailor resume**, **Write cover letter** or **Practice interview** for this role.
- **People:** add people at the company (see [Reaching people](#7-reaching-people)).
- **Details:** fix the company, title, place, link or posting text. **Pay from** and **Pay to** take pay in any form, like `17/hr`, `45k a year`, `3,000 a month` or a range like `17-19/hr`. It's shown both ways, like "$62,000–$74,000 a year (about $30–$36 an hour)". If a role pays in a different currency from your profile, the pay check says unknown, not fail.

Jobs posted more than 45 days ago drop out of the way on their own.

## 6. Resumes and cover letters

1. On a role, choose **Tailor resume** or **Write cover letter**. It takes a minute or two, and the page updates when it's ready.
2. The document opens with a list of **Checks**:
   - every claim comes from a fact in your profile, and each resume line cites facts from its own job
   - no tool you marked "never used" appears
   - the page count: one or two pages for a resume, one for a cover letter
   - your name, email and phone can be read by applicant tracking systems, the software employers use to scan resumes
   - a cover letter is under 350 words
   - a second AI check confirms the claims match your facts

   Under the checks it may list words from the posting that your profile can't honestly support. They're left out on purpose. If you do have them, add them to your profile and make the document again.
3. **Read it yourself.** In the **Edit** part, change any line (each one shows the profile facts it relies on), then choose **Rebuild PDF and re-check**. Clear a line's text to remove it.
4. **View PDF** to see it, then **Download** it. The file is named after you, the document and the company, like `Pat_Lee_Resume_Acme.pdf`. Attach it to your application.
5. **Approve** it when you're happy with it. If a check didn't pass, the app asks you to confirm first.

Documents use US Letter paper. For A4, change **Paper size** in Settings.

When you apply, set the role's status to **Applied**, so Today can remind you to follow up.

## 7. Reaching people

A word from someone inside the company often matters more than the application itself. On a role, under **People**, choose **Add a person**: a recruiter, a future manager, or a friend of a friend. Set **Relationship** to **Warm** if you already know them, so the role shows you have a way in, or **Cold** if you haven't met.

- Choose the kind of message you want drafted:
  - a LinkedIn connection note (kept under LinkedIn's 200-character limit)
  - a LinkedIn InMail
  - an email
  - a follow-up
- The draft uses only your real experience. Edit it until it sounds like you and choose **Save edits**. Then use **Copy** and paste it into LinkedIn or your email yourself. Job Search HQ never sends anything.
- Choose **I sent it**. It reminds you to follow up in a week. Later, choose **they replied** or **no reply**.

The **Outreach** page lists everyone you're in touch with and who's due a follow-up.

## 8. Interview practice

1. Open **Practice** and choose **Give me a question**, or type your own question first. Or choose **Practice interview** on a role to get questions about that job.
2. Answer:
   - **Out loud:** choose **Record answer**, speak as you would in the interview, then **Stop and grade**. Aim for one to two minutes (five at most). Your recording is turned into text and then deleted. On some servers voice is turned off, and you type instead.
   - **Typed:** write your answer, then choose **Grade my answer**.
3. You get a score out of 20: up to 5 points each for structure (situation, action, result), evidence (specific numbers and examples), fit to the role, and length (one to two minutes spoken is ideal). You also see what worked, one thing to fix, and a likely follow-up question. Try again and watch the score change.

## 9. Chat

The **Chat** page is for anything quick. You can type things like:
- "What should I do today?"
- "Acme moved my interview to Thursday at 2."
- "Remind me to email Jordan on Monday at 9."
- "Which of my roles are remote and pay over 120k?"

Changes it makes to your roles and reminders show an **Undo** button. Some changes are yours alone:
- **It can't mark you hired** (Accepted), and **it can't reopen a role** that's rejected, withdrawn, skipped or otherwise closed. If you ask, it tells you to do it yourself with the status buttons on the role page.
- **A role it adds** for you starts as saved or applied.
- **It can't change your profile directly.** A fact it learns goes to Suggestions for your approval.

## 10. Inbox

The **Inbox** page shows what came in from Gmail and what the app did with it. **Check Gmail now** checks right away instead of waiting for the hourly check.

- **Job alerts** add new roles.
- **Emails from an employer** can update a role on their own: for example to "applied" when they confirm your application, "interviewing" when they invite you to talk, "offer", or "rejected" when they turn you down. This only happens when the email clearly comes from that company:
  - from the company's own domain, whose name is the company's whole name (`acme.com` or `acme.co.uk` for Acme, but not a look-alike such as `acme-careers.com`), or a domain you've recorded for it: the one in the role's link, or a contact's work email
  - or from a hiring system such as Greenhouse, Lever or Workday, when the company's name is also in the sender's name or the subject
- **Everything else waits for you to review**, with no change made. That includes scheduling and test sites anyone can sign up for (Calendly, HackerRank, Google Forms and the like). The Inbox says why, for example "Sender … isn't Acme or a hiring system naming it" or "(no matching role)".
- **It never** marks you as hired, never reopens a role you closed ("not reopening it"), and never moves a role backwards, for example from interviewing back to applied ("this email would move it backwards").
- **Every change has Undo.** Use **Dismiss** to clear something you've dealt with.

**Companies to watch:** many companies list their jobs on Greenhouse, Lever or Ashby. Open the company's careers page, and if its address starts with `boards.greenhouse.io`, `jobs.lever.co` or `jobs.ashbyhq.com`, you can watch it:
1. Enter the company name and paste that careers link.
2. Add a few **title keywords**, like `marketing, operations`.
3. Choose **Watch**.

Each morning, new openings whose titles match are added and scored.

## 11. Reminders and notifications

- **Set a reminder** on the Reminders page, or in Chat ("remind me tomorrow at 9 to call Sam"). When it's due you get a notification with **Done** and **Snooze 1 hour** buttons. The Reminders page lists what's coming up, where you can cancel any of them. It also lists what was sent recently, where you can mark one done, snooze it an hour, or move it to tomorrow at 9 AM.
- **The morning summary:** at 7:30 AM you get a short notification of what's due today.
- **Inbox news:** you're also notified when an email changes a role.

Times use the time zone your copy was set up with.

## 12. Settings and your data

- **Claude:** choose how your copy reaches Claude. There are two options:
  - **My own Claude plan** uses your Claude Pro or Max plan. The person who runs your server signs Claude Code in once with your Claude account, and then you choose **Save** here. Job Search HQ never sees your Claude password.
  - **An API key** is pay per use. Paste the key, set a monthly budget, then **Save**.

  **Test Claude** checks either one, and **Recent AI use** shows what it has been doing. **Model** picks which Claude model does the work.
- **Paper size:** US Letter or A4, for your resumes and cover letters.
- **Signed-in devices:** see where you're signed in. **Sign out everywhere** if you lose a device.
- **Download my data:** a zip file with a spreadsheet (CSV) for each kind of record: roles and their timelines, people and messages, your work history, education, facts and skills, suggestions, documents, practice, reminders, inbox items, chats and the change history. It also has your basic information (`profile.json`) and the PDF of every resume and cover letter. Saved keys and tokens are never included. Download it when your search is over.

**Your privacy:**
- **Separate copies:** your copy is yours alone, with its own encrypted database. Even when other people use the same server, nobody else's copy can see your data.
- **The person who runs the server** looks after the machine. The app never shows them your data, but as with any server, whoever controls it could get at it, so pick someone you trust.
- **What reaches the AI:** only what a task needs from your own copy, such as your profile, a role and its posting.

## 13. If something goes wrong

| What you see | What to do |
|---|---|
| "This copy of Job Search HQ belongs to someone else" or "This Google account isn't the one that set up this copy" | You're signed in to Chrome with a different Google account. Sign out of Google, or use a different Chrome profile, and sign in with the right account. |
| "Your session ended. Sign in again." | Sign-ins end after a week without use, after 30 days in any case, or after **Sign out everywhere**. Sign in again. |
| "That sign-in link expired or was already used" or "This sign-in was started in a different browser" | Start again from **Sign in with Google**, in the same browser window, and finish within 10 minutes. |
| "Google hasn't verified this app" | Expected. Choose **Continue**, or **Advanced**, then **Go to … (unsafe)**. |
| "Access blocked: … has not completed the Google verification process" | Your Google account isn't on the server's list of test users yet. Ask the person who runs your server to add it (install.md, step 4, Part 3). |
| "Gmail wasn't connected: the box to allow reading email was left unticked" | Choose **Connect Gmail** again, and tick the box on Google's screen. |
| "Google didn't give lasting access" | Remove Job Search HQ at [myaccount.google.com/permissions](https://myaccount.google.com/permissions), then choose **Connect Gmail** again. |
| "Gmail is disconnected" on Today | Normal about once a week: Google ends access to private apps every 7 days. Choose **Reconnect Gmail**. It takes a few seconds, and it picks up the emails that came in while it was off. |
| Gmail stopped bringing in jobs | Look for **Reconnect Gmail** on Today, or open Setup and choose **Connect Gmail** again. |
| No notifications | On Setup, choose **Send a test**. If nothing arrives, check that notifications for the site or app are allowed in the device's settings, then choose **Turn on notifications** again. |
| "Connect Claude in Settings first" or "Claude isn't connected yet" | Claude hasn't been set up for your copy. See [Settings](#12-settings-and-your-data). |
| "Claude rejected the sign-in" or "Claude Code isn't signed in" | Ask the person who runs your server to sign Claude in again, or paste a new API key in Settings. |
| "The Anthropic account is out of credit" | Add credit to the API key's account at console.anthropic.com. |
| "This month's AI budget is used up" | Raise the monthly budget in Settings, or wait until next month. |
| "Claude is busy right now" | Try again in a minute. |
| "Saved, but the posting couldn't be fetched" | The role was saved without its posting. Copy the posting text from the job site and paste it into **Posting text** on the role page. |
| A pay or commute message, such as "Enter an amount like 18/hr, 45k a year or 3,000 a month" | Type the amount with its unit, as in the message. For a range, put the lower amount first and use one currency. A commute is a number of miles, like `15`, or kilometers, like `25 km`. |
| "The server isn't responding; it may be restarting" | Wait a minute and try again. The server restarts after updates. |
| "Can't reach the server" or a "You're offline" page | Check your Wi-Fi or mobile data, then choose **Try again**. Your data is safe on your server. |
| A document is taking a long time | AI tasks take a minute or two, and on a small server they wait in line. Leave the page open, or come back later. |
| Something changed that you didn't want | Look for the **Undo** button next to it on Inbox or Chat, or in **Change history** on the role page. |

## 14. Making it work for your search

Every kind of search uses the same tools. What changes is what you put in your profile and where your jobs come from. Find the description closest to yours.

**First job, internship or recent graduate**
- **Work history:** add internships, part-time and campus jobs and volunteer roles as jobs.
- **Projects:** add big school, club or personal projects under **Projects**, with what you did and what came of it ("Led a team of four that built a budgeting app used by 200 students").
- **Education, certifications and licenses:** add them on the Profile page. With a short work history, your resume puts them first. Put a strong GPA or awards under **Other facts**.
- **Target level:** "entry level" or "internship".
- **The Interview page** is especially useful: it draws out experience you may not think counts.
- **Practice:** early interviews lean on questions like "Tell me about a time you worked on a team". Practice them until your score is steady.

**Hourly, shift or frontline work** (retail, warehouse, food service, health care support, the trades, driving)
- **Pay:** enter an hourly amount, like `18/hr`.
- **Work modes and commute:** choose **On-site** and set **Longest commute**, like `15` (miles) or `25 km`, so far-away jobs fail the location check.
- **Schedule:** put the shifts and days you can work in **Anything else the AI should know** ("weekdays only, no overnight shifts").
- **Licenses and certifications:** add each one, such as a CNA license, food handler card, CPR or forklift certification, under **Certifications and licenses**. They all appear on your resume.
- **Alerts:** Indeed often has more of these jobs than LinkedIn, so set up Indeed alerts as well.
- **Documents:** many of these applications want a resume only. Tailor the resume and skip the cover letter unless one is asked for.

**Returning after a break** (caregiving, health, study, a layoff)
- **Work history:** add your past jobs as normal. Add anything from the break that counts, such as volunteering, freelance work, courses or caring responsibilities you'd mention in an interview.
- **The break:** say briefly what it was in **Anything else the AI should know**, so drafts handle it plainly instead of hiding it.
- **Practice:** rehearse a short, honest answer to "Tell me about the gap in your resume" until it feels natural.

**Changing careers**
- **Facts:** write results in terms the new field understands: what you did, for whom, and the result, with numbers.
- **Courses and certifications** for the new field go under **Certifications and licenses**, and work you did to learn it under **Projects**.
- **Tools:** the Interview page asks about tools the jobs you've saved mention. Answer honestly. **Never used it** keeps a tool out of your documents, so nothing claims what you can't back up.
- **The score:** each fit score names the main gap. If the same gap keeps coming up, that's what to learn next.
- **People:** in a career change, introductions matter even more. Use **Reaching people** on the roles you care about most.

**Mid-career**
- **Facts with numbers** make the biggest difference: money saved or earned, time cut, people led, customers served.
- **Warm paths:** add anyone you know at a company as **Warm**, so the role shows you have a way in.

**Senior and executive roles**
- **Many roles are never posted.** Watch the companies you'd most like to join (on the Inbox page). On their roles, add recruiters and leaders under **People**.
- **Roles a recruiter brings you:** add them with what you know in **Notes**, then choose **Score fit**. They're scored without a posting ([section 4](#4-where-jobs-come-from)).
- **Target level and pay:** set both. The level shapes each fit score, and the pay check flags roles below your floor.
- **Facts:** lead with scope and outcomes: the size of the team, the budget, the revenue, the turnaround.

**Searching outside the US**
- **Pay** can be in your own currency, like `€45k` or `C$60k`, and **commute** in kilometers, like `25 km`.
- **Paper size:** choose A4 in Settings.
- **Time zone:** reminders use the time zone your copy was set up with. If it's wrong, ask the person who runs your server.

**Jobs from sites it doesn't read** (government sites such as USAJOBS, school districts, hospitals, local job boards)
- On Roles, choose **Add a role** and paste the posting text into **Posting text**. Everything else then works as normal: the score, the checks, the documents and practice.

**If typing is hard**
- **Practice out loud:** record answers instead of typing them, where voice is turned on.
- **Speak instead of type** anywhere in the app, using your device's own dictation:
  - **Windows:** press **Windows key + H**.
  - **Mac:** choose **Edit → Start Dictation**, or press the dictation key (the microphone key, or **Fn** twice, depending on your settings).
  - **Chromebook:** turn on dictation in **Settings → Accessibility → Keyboard and text input**, then press **Search + D**.
  - **iPhone, iPad and Android:** tap the microphone on the keyboard.
- **Make text bigger:** **Ctrl + plus** (Windows, Chromebook), **⌘ Command + plus** (Mac), or pinch on a phone.
